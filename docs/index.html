The Lifetime One-Shot Rule

Unlike states with rolling expungements, Indiana permits conviction expungement only once in your lifetime. Every eligible conviction across all 92 counties must be consolidated and filed within the statutory window.

“A person may file a petition for expungement of a conviction record under this chapter only once in their lifetime.”

— Indiana Code § 35-38-9-9(i)
RULE 01

Single Lifetime Proceeding

Indiana strictly limits conviction record expungement to a single proceeding in your lifetime. You cannot expunge a misdemeanor today and file for a felony years later. All eligible convictions must be handled at once.

RULE 02

365-Day Window

Under IC § 35-38-9-9(d), if you have convictions in different Indiana counties (e.g., Marion and Huntington), all subsequent petitions must be filed within 365 days of the first county filing.

RULE 03

Permanent Forfeiture

If you omit any conviction from your petition, you permanently forfeit the right to ever expunge that conviction for the remainder of your life. Omitted records remain public forever.

Filing Logistics

Costs, Venues & Service

Court filing fees, county clerk filing, statutory service recipients, and pre-filing record verification.

How much does an expungement cost in Indiana if I represent myself?

Filing Fees: Under Indiana law, petitions for non-convictions (arrests, dismissals, dropped charges, acquittals) require $0 in court filing fees.

For convictions (misdemeanors or felonies), standard Indiana trial courts charge a single civil filing fee (typically ~$157), regardless of how many cases you consolidate in that county. If you receive public assistance or have low income, you can submit the included DOC 08 Fee Waiver Petition to ask the judge to waive this fee entirely under IC § 33-37-3-2.

Where do I send and file my completed expungement documents?

Under Indiana Trial Rules and the expungement statute, your paperwork must be directed to specific recipients:

  • The Trial Court (County Clerk): File your original Appearance Form (DOC 01), Notice of Exclusion & Confidential Sheet (DOC 02 & 03), Verified Petition (DOC 04), Proposed Order (DOC 07), and optional Fee Waiver (DOC 08) with the Circuit or Superior Court Clerk in the county where your conviction occurred. This can be done in person at the clerk's window or through the Indiana E-Filing System (IEFS).
  • The Prosecuting Attorney: Serve a copy of the petition and Notice of Filing (DOC 05) on the County Prosecuting Attorney via Certified U.S. Mail or IEFS e-service. The prosecutor has 30 days to review the petition and file an objection or consent.
  • State Repositories (ISP & BMV): When the judge grants and signs your Proposed Order (DOC 07), certified copies must be served on the Indiana State Police Criminal History Repository and the Bureau of Motor Vehicles so central databases are officially sealed and driver records updated.
  • Local Law Enforcement: The signed Order must also be served on the County Sheriff and arresting police agencies to seal local arrest logs and detention records.
Do I need an official background check before filing?

Yes, it is strongly recommended. While MyCase contains most public court records, relying solely on it can be risky. Indiana allows conviction expungement only once in your lifetime.

If an older case was archived, expunged previously but incorrectly recorded, or originated in a county before they transitioned to MyCase, it might not appear in a standard search. Obtaining an official Indiana State Police (ISP) Criminal History Background Check ensures every charge is accounted for before generating your final packet.

Is my personal data or court history ever uploaded to a server or cloud database?

Never. The entire assistant runs 100% locally in your browser. The tool parses public records directly from your active MyCase session, and all 10 PDF court forms are compiled on your machine using pdf-lib.

There are zero external tracking servers, zero cloud databases, and zero analytics scripts capturing your data. Your criminal history, SSN, and personal identifiers remain strictly on your computer.

Statutory Standards

Eligibility, Rules & Rights

Mandatory versus discretionary standards, the lifetime one-shot limit, and post-expungement legal protections.

What is the difference between "Mandatory" and "Discretionary" expungements?

For non-convictions, misdemeanors, and non-violent Level 6 / Class D felonies without bodily injury, Indiana law specifies that the trial court SHALL grant the expungement if you meet the statutory prerequisites (waiting period passed, fines paid, no pending charges). The judge has no legal discretion to deny a compliant petition.

For higher-level major felonies (Levels 1–5), the statute provides that the court MAY grant the petition. The trial judge weighs evidence of rehabilitation, character letters, and prosecutor objections before making a discretionary ruling.

What happens if I accidentally forget to include a case in my petition?

Under Indiana Code § 35-38-9-9, you can expunge conviction records only once in your lifetime. Any conviction omitted from your petition is permanently forfeited and can never be expunged in the future.

Furthermore, if you have convictions across multiple Indiana counties, all petitions must be filed within a coordinated 365-day statutory window. To prevent irreversible mistakes, our assistant requires completing a mandatory 4-point pre-filing audit checklist (verifying maiden names, aliases, driving history, and all 92 Indiana counties) before document generation is unlocked.

Can traffic infractions, speeding tickets, or driver's license points be expunged?

No. Expungement only applies to criminal offenses (arrests, misdemeanors, and felonies). Civil traffic infractions (IF cause numbers on MyCase, like standard speeding tickets) are not criminal offenses and cannot be expunged under this law.

However, criminal traffic offenses—such as Operating While Intoxicated (OWI/DUI), Driving While Suspended (Class A Misdemeanor), or Reckless Driving—are criminal convictions and can be fully expunged once statutory waiting periods are met.

Can an employer or landlord ask about or discriminate against an expunged record?

Under Indiana Code § 35-38-9-10, it is unlawful discrimination for an employer, licensing board, or housing provider to discriminate against an individual based on an expunged record.

Furthermore, job applications in Indiana cannot ask about expunged records, and you may legally state under Indiana law that the arrest or conviction never occurred.

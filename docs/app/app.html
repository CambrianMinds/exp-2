How to Save Records from MyCase (iPhone, Android, PC / Mac) Device Instructions

Search your legal name and any former names or aliases across all 92 counties on public.courts.in.gov/mycase. While viewing your search results or individual Case Summary, save the page as described below and upload the saved file:

iPhone / iPad Safari
  1. On the MyCase page, tap the Share button (square with arrow pointing up).
  2. Tap Options at the top of the share sheet, select Web Archive (or PDF), and tap Done.
  3. Tap Save to Files, choose your Downloads or Documents folder, and tap Save.
  4. Return here and tap Choose File(s) to select that file from your Files app.
Android Chrome / Browser
  1. On the MyCase page, tap the Menu (three dots ⋮ in the upper right).
  2. Tap Share, choose Print, change destination to Save as PDF, and tap the PDF save button.
  3. Alternatively, tap the Download icon in the menu to save the page offline.
  4. Return here and tap Choose File(s) to upload the saved file from your device.
PC / Mac / Linux Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari
  1. On the MyCase search results page, press Ctrl + S (or Cmd + S on Mac).
  2. Select Webpage, Complete or Webpage, HTML Only and click Save.
  3. Drag and drop that downloaded .html file straight into the upload box below.
  4. Fastest Shortcut: Or drag the 1-Click Bookmarklet below to your bookmarks bar for 1-click JSON export.

Why This Project Exists

Indiana's Second Chance Law looks great on paper, but the vocabulary we use around it is doing enormous, unhelpful work. The state feels empathy for people with records, so it wrote a statute. But empathy is just a feeling; actual care is resource allocation. Right now, the state's empathy is locked behind a gate with up to a $3,000 admission fee, operated by private attorneys. It is an economic filter that locks out the exact people who need a clean slate the most.

It is not absolution. It is the raw infrastructure required to do the work yourself — the same work an attorney charges thousands to format on your behalf. The law belongs to you. This is just the machinery to use it.

Crucial One-Shot Rule Warning (IC § 35-38-9-9(i))

Under Indiana Code § 35-38-9-9(i): “A person may file a petition for expungement of a conviction record under this chapter only one (1) time during any period of the person's life.”

IF YOU OMIT ANY CONVICTION, YOU CAN NEVER EXPUNGE IT LATER. All petitions across different counties must also be filed within 365 days (IC § 35-38-9-9(d)).

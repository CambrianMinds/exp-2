What Does Indiana Expungement Do? (Tiers & Limits) Plain Guide
Section 1 · IC § 35-38-9-1

Non-Convictions / Arrests

FULL SEALING

Permanently sealed from public view. Treated as if the arrest never occurred. $0 filing fee. No lifetime limit.

Sections 2–3 · IC § 35-38-9-2 & 3

Misdemeanors & Level 6 Felonies

MANDATORY SEALING

Court must grant if statutory time met (5 yrs CM / 8 yrs L6). Sealed from public and private employers. Restores civil rights.

Sections 4–5 · IC § 35-38-9-4 & 5

Major Felonies

DISCRETIONARY / MARKED EXPUNGED

Court discretion. Dockets marked "Expunged / Civil Rights Restored". Level 5+ serious bodily injury requires prosecutor consent.

Statutorily Barred Offenses:

Homicide/death (IC § 35-42-1), sex registry offenses (IC § 11-8-8), human trafficking (IC § 35-42-3.5), public corruption by elected officials, 2+ weapons felonies, or active pending charges.

Why Is Full Address History Required?

Under IC § 35-38-9-8(b)(3), your petition must list every residential address from your earliest arrest/offense to today:

  • Statewide Background Check: Prosecutors verify no open warrants or pending cases exist where you previously lived.
  • Sealed on Form ACR: Address history is filed confidentially under Access to Court Records Rule 5 (not viewable by public).
  • Prevents Objections: Leaving off a prior residence is a statutory pleading defect that can delay your filing.

Filing Logistics & When to Consult Counsel

Pro se filing steps, service methods, and complex cases requiring attorney advice

1. Filing Methods: IEFS vs. In-Person

• Odyssey E-Filing System (IEFS): Free pro se registration through certified service providers (e.g. fileandserve.courts.in.gov). Upload generated PDFs; e-service automatically notifies the County Prosecutor.
• In-Person Clerk Filing: Print 4 copies of your complete petition packet. Present to County Clerk with filing fee or Form 08 Fee Waiver. Have Clerk date-stamp your retain copies.

2. What to Expect After Filing (Timelines)

Under IC § 35-38-9-8(f), the Prosecuting Attorney has 30 days to file an objection. If no objection is filed, the court may enter the order without a hearing.

Complex Cases Where You Should Consult an Attorney:
  • Out-of-state or federal convictions that might impact statutory waiting periods
  • Contested victim restitution amounts or missing payment satisfactions
  • Class C/B/A or Level 5/4/3/2/1 felonies where the prosecutor may object
  • Professional licensing boards (medical, nursing, legal) with mandatory reporting rules
